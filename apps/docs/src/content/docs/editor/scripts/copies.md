title: Rename or duplicate a script
description: Update a title and subtitle, or make a separate draft with the options you need.
---

Rename `The door` when its working title changes. Duplicate it when you want a separate draft for rehearsals.

## Rename a script

1. On the home screen, find its card.
2. Open the <img class="kb-editor-icon" src="/icons/editor/script-actions.svg" alt="" aria-hidden="true" /> three-dot menu, **Open actions for The door**.
3. Choose **Rename script**.
4. Set **Title** to `The last door`. Add or change **Subtitle** if needed.
5. Choose **Save changes**.

The same script appears under its updated title and subtitle. Its writing stays in place. Title and subtitle are also used by the title page; its other fields, such as credits, are edited in [Script settings](/editor/getting-started/#where-to-find-script-settings).

## Make a rehearsal draft

1. Open the card's three-dot menu and choose **Duplicate script**.
2. Set **Title** to `The last door — rehearsal draft`.
3. Turn on **Copy settings** and **Copy attributes** if you want them in the draft.
4. Select **Open in editor after creating** to start working on the copy immediately.
5. Choose **Duplicate script**.

A separate script is created. Later changes to its writing do not update the original.

## Choose what to copy

Script content, including acts, scenes, places and musical entries, is always copied. The two switches are initially off:

| Option | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Copy settings | Keeps page layout, block styles and flow settings, initial-page settings, headers and footers. Otherwise the copy uses defaults. |
| Copy attributes | Keeps confirmed characters and groups with their details. Without it, names in the copied writing are unconfirmed. |

The copy starts without the original subtitle or title-page details. Music attachments and comments are not copied by this action. Check the new script before using it as your rehearsal draft.

Use a [`.stepkg` file](/editor/scripts/download/) when you want a portable backup with metadata and attachments. Duplicating a draft and restoring a package serve different needs.
